# Portfolio Management Framework

## Separate portfolio purposes
Keep long-term core holdings, benchmark allocations, and speculative/trading positions distinct when the user's plan does so.

## Allocation math
- Weight (%) = position market value / total portfolio market value × 100
- Target gap (percentage points) = current weight − target weight
- Target value = target weight × portfolio market value
- Value gap = target value − current position value

State whether percentages are based on invested assets, total account value, or a wider household balance sheet. Never mix denominators.

## Concentration and overlap
Review:
- Largest positions and top-5/top-10 weights
- Sector and industry exposure
- Shared revenue drivers (e.g., cloud capex, AI infrastructure, semiconductors)
- Geographic and currency exposure
- Sensitivity to rates, economic cycles, regulation, and commodity prices

A portfolio can be diversified by ticker but concentrated by underlying economic driver.

## Rebalancing discipline
Compare the benefit of rebalancing with taxes, fees, spreads, FX, and the risk of interrupting compounding. Consider directing new contributions toward underweight positions before selling overweight holdings. Avoid unnecessary trades for small deviations.

## Decision framework
Offer choices with trade-offs:
1. Hold / monitor
2. Direct new contributions
3. Partial rebalance
4. Full rebalance

Specify conditions that would trigger reconsideration. Never assume the target weights are automatically optimal or must be reached immediately.

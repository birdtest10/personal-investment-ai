# Fundamental Analysis Framework

## Business understanding
- What does the company sell, to whom, and why do customers pay?
- Identify revenue segments, geographic mix, customer concentration, pricing power, recurring vs cyclical revenue.
- Map the value chain and identify who captures the economics.

## Competitive advantage
Assess evidence for brand, network effects, switching costs, scale, cost advantage, IP/data, distribution, or regulatory barriers. A moat claim must explain how it protects returns and for how long.

## Financial quality
Review 3–5 years where available:
- Revenue and organic growth
- Gross, operating, and net margins
- EPS and share-count changes
- Operating cash flow and free cash flow
- Capex and working capital
- Cash, debt, interest coverage, maturities
- ROIC/ROE and reinvestment needs

Check whether earnings convert to cash. Reconcile adjusted metrics with GAAP/reported figures. Note cyclicality and unusual items.

## Growth quality
Distinguish volume, price, mix, acquisitions, and FX. Examine customer retention, backlog/RPO, utilization, capacity, unit economics, and incremental margins where relevant.

## Management and capital allocation
Assess execution against prior guidance, dilution/SBC, buybacks, dividends, M&A, debt reduction, and reinvestment returns.

## Questions to answer
1. What is the strongest reason this business can compound value?
2. What evidence supports that claim?
3. What could structurally weaken it?
4. What key metric would reveal deterioration early?

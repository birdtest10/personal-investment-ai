# Valuation Methods

## Choose a method that fits the business
- P/E: profitable companies with reasonably interpretable earnings; adjust for cycles and unusual items.
- Forward P/E: depends on forecast quality; show the estimate source and date.
- EV/EBITDA: useful for comparing capital structures, but weak when capex needs differ materially.
- EV/Sales: early-stage or low/negative-profit firms; pair with margin and path-to-profitability analysis.
- P/FCF: useful when cash flow is meaningful and not distorted by working capital/capex timing.
- DCF: useful when cash flows can be forecast with defensible assumptions; highly sensitive to inputs.
- Sum-of-the-parts: conglomerates or materially different business segments.

## DCF discipline
Show:
- Forecast period and revenue growth assumptions
- Operating margin and tax assumptions
- D&A, capex, and working-capital assumptions
- Free cash flow definition
- Discount rate / WACC rationale
- Terminal growth or exit multiple rationale
- Net debt, diluted share count, and equity value bridge

Use bear/base/bull cases when possible. Run sensitivity on major assumptions. Do not present a DCF point estimate as objective truth.

## Valuation output
- Current price and timestamp/source
- Relevant multiples and comparison set
- Fair-value range, not just a single target
- Implied upside/downside by scenario
- Margin of safety and key assumptions
- Reasons the market may rationally assign a premium/discount

Never fabricate consensus, target prices, or financial inputs. If data is missing, provide a model outline and list required inputs.
